# Finding the niche angle

Generic sets come from generic briefs. Before writing a single headline, find the **angle**: the one way
this app is *for someone specific*. The angle drives the copy *and* the visual direction - it is the
difference between "a TV tracker" and "the tracker for people who'd rather not watch what everyone watches".

## 1. Five angle types

| Angle | Question | Library examples |
|---|---|---|
| **The exact person** | Who is this *really* for, narrower than the category? | Working Copy (developers who want real Git on iPhone), Structured (people who plan their day in time blocks), LADDER (people who lift) |
| **The enemy** | What does the app refuse that the category accepts? | TickPick "No hidden fees. Ever.", Bible "No Ads or Purchases", Seenfy "Not what's popular", Ground News "bias" |
| **The ritual** | Which recurring moment does it own? | HIRKA "Cardigan or coat? One glance." (the morning), Waze (the commute), Seenfy "Bu akşam ne izlesem?" (the evening) |
| **The insider word** | Which words does this crowd use that outsiders don't? | Working Copy "merge conflicts", "commit graph"; Seenfy "S2E5", "plot twist", "watchlist"; Tunesimo "tune twins", "repeaters" |
| **The proof only you have** | What number, feature or data can no competitor show? | Indeed "30% faster", Kikoff "+25pts", WeatherX "rain timing: start to finish", Seenfy "%88 Sana Uygun" match score |

Most strong sets combine two: an enemy in shot 1, then insider words and proof in shots 2–5.

## 2. Writing three angles and choosing one

From the listing (or the user's answers) and the screens, write **three one-line angles**, each of a
different type, then pick the one that:
- the screens can **prove** (no angle without a screen behind it),
- competitors in the category **can't say** (run the swap test on the angle itself),
- the audience would **repeat to a friend**.

Put the chosen angle in the brief as a sixth line: `Angle: <type> - <one line>`. Every headline should
serve it; at least shot 1 must state it.

Example - Seenfy:
- Exact person: "for people who keep a film diary" (proof: lists, ratings - weaker on screen)
- Enemy: "not what's popular - what you'll love" (proof: "%88 Sana Uygun" match) ← chosen
- Ritual: "the end of 'ne izlesek?' every evening" (proof: Discover, collections)

## 3. From angle to direction

The angle suggests the visual system. Start here, then confirm with the category playbook
(`categories.md`) and the brand.

| Angle feels… | Directions | Why |
|---|---|---|
| curated, taste, expertise | `editorial`, `cinematic` | serif and restraint read as judgement |
| energy, deals, speed, appetite | `poster` | huge type and colour flood read as confidence |
| community, fun, belonging | `playful` | rounded type and stickers read as people |
| immersion, mood, content | `cinematic` | the app's own imagery becomes the ground |
| care, local, everyday ritual | `crafted` | warmth and a motif read as made-by-hand |
| competence, clarity | `native` | big clean UI reads as "it just works" |
| power, data, precision | `utility` | grids, mono and numbers read as control |

Show **three** directions with `concepts.py` - usually one safe (fits the category), one bold, one
unexpected for the category. The unexpected one is where niche sets often come from: an editorial set in
a category of loud posters stands out in search.

## 4. Niche visual moves

A direction gives the system; one ownable detail makes it *this* app's. Pick one, use it on every shot:
- **A motif from the product:** Moovit's transit lines, HIRKA's knitted rope, a film strip or ticket stub
  for a cinema app, a stitched seam for a fashion app. (`crafted` → `"shapes"` with `wave`/`line`.)
- **The app's own UI element as a sticker:** Seenfy's "%88 Sana Uygun" badge, a streak flame, a "S2E5" chip
  (`"stickers"` or a callout).
- **Colour from the content:** let the posters, maps or photos colour the ground (`cinematic` samples the
  screen).
- **Insider typography:** mono for developers, condensed sports caps for betting/fantasy, a bookish serif
  for readers.
- **Hand marks only where a human would point:** circle the one thing the headline talks about
  (`"annotations"`), never decorate for its own sake.

## 5. Checklist

- [ ] The brief has an `Angle:` line of one of the five types.
- [ ] Shot 1 states the angle; it fails the swap test for every competitor.
- [ ] At least two headlines use the audience's insider words.
- [ ] The direction was chosen from 3 concepts, with a reason tied to the angle and the category.
- [ ] One ownable visual detail repeats across the set.
